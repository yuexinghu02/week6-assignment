About the data

Source: DHS Office of Homeland Security Statistics, Key Homeland Security Metric: USCG Search and Rescue Responses (workbook: Search and Rescue Responses KHSM 12.17.24.xlsx), built from the Coast Guard's MISLE system. DHS rounds every row to the nearest 10, so small zones and totals built from many rows carry rounding error; national figures here are summed from zone rows and can differ slightly from DHS's published totals. Zone-level monthly figures come from the monthly sheet, annual figures from the annual sheet.

Save rate is lives saved divided by lives saved plus lives lost after the Coast Guard was notified or arrived, plus people unaccounted for. Lives lost before notification are left out because the Coast Guard could not affect them. Change per year is the least-squares slope across FY2020–2024 as a percent of the zone's average. Lives on the line counts everyone saved, lost (before or after notification) or unaccounted for. Busy season vs normal month is the busiest month's average divided by the zone's monthly average. "Top 10 in" counts how many of the five questions a zone ranks in the top 10 for among all 35 zones; for the change-per-year question only rising zones count, and for share saved only zones with at least 50 people a year are ranked.

The data has no unit-level staffing, asset or station detail, so this shows where demand is, not how well each zone is resourced against it.
